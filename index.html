About

I'm an electrical and computer engineer from Karachi. I started university at 17 on a full-tuition merit scholarship at Habib University, studied electrical engineering, and kept gravitating toward the parts of the field where software meets real hardware — microcontrollers, sensors, control systems, and robots that have to work in the messy physical world.

Along the way I spent a summer as a fully-funded research fellow at Purdue University, built a self-balancing robot and a vision-guided robotic arm, published my final-year work in IEEE, and took a fish-waste-to-biodiesel idea to a global second-place finish at Texas A&M. After graduating I moved into industry at House of Habib — battery-chemistry research, a locally-designed transformer oil-level sensor, and a megawatt-scale solar installation among other projects.

It paid well and it was interesting. But I missed the hands-on research and teaching, so I came back to Habib University as a Research Assistant. Now I teach the microcontroller and robotics labs, help mentor competition teams, and I'm working toward graduate research in embedded and cyber-physical systems. If you're looking for someone who is genuinely excited to build, break, and understand hardware — that's the whole reason I'm here.

Featured work

The four I'd most want you to look at.

Final Year Project · IEEE publication

Smart Energy Management for Renewable-Heavy Grids

A Python-based platform that helps distribution-network operators absorb more renewable energy. It handles real-time data, automated modelling, and visualisation to support hosting-capacity analysis and voltage control, using optimisation to keep losses low and voltage stable — validated against IEEE standards.

The work became a conference paper on IEEE Xplore and two accepted posters at the DSSE Undergraduate Research Symposium (DURS) 2024.

IEEE Xplore paper → Project video →

Purdue University · SURF 2023

Origami-Inspired Planetary Exploration Robot

As a fully-funded summer research fellow at Purdue, I helped build an autonomous, multi-modal locomotive robot for space exploration — an origami-inspired structure that changes how it moves across terrain, driven by an Arduino microcontroller. I presented the project as a poster at the SURF symposium.

Research paper → SURF certificates →

Invent For The Planet · 2nd place globally

FinDiesel — Fish Waste to Biodiesel

A portable kit that turns fish waste into biodiesel, cutting fuel costs for fishing boats in Pakistan and other developing economies — a step toward energy self-sufficiency for coastal communities. We won the Habib University round and placed 2nd out of 35 universities worldwide in the global finals hosted by Texas A&M.

Power Systems · IEEE publication

Load Flow with Smart-Inverter Voltage Control

Traditional power-flow analysis ignores what modern smart inverters actually do. I extended the classic load-flow algorithm on an IEEE 14-bus network to model smart inverters running in Volt-VAR and Volt-WATT control modes — adjusting the Jacobian and power injections so the analysis reflects how renewable-driven grids really behave under fluctuation and disturbance.

Beyond the lab

The things that shaped how I think about engineering.

U.S.–Pakistan energy policy, in the room

As a member of the Pakistan-U.S. Alumni Network (PUAN), I was selected to attend Powering Progress: U.S.–Pakistan Synergy in Energy and Mineral Resources in Islamabad, alongside academics, policymakers, and industry leaders. We discussed Pakistan's underused mineral and energy potential, post-18th-Amendment governance, and how to make the grid robust enough for large-scale renewable integration — the exact challenge I'd explored in my capstone. I also visited NEECA to hear their plans for national EV-charging infrastructure.

Buraq Space Camp, at 16

I went to a government college to save money — and still got selected as one of 40 students, out of more than 5,000 nationwide, for the prestigious Buraq Space Camp in 2018: ten residential days in Islamabad packed with lectures, activities, and meetings with professors. The highlight was meeting Dr. Pervez Hoodbhoy.

Fun fact: last year I helped build the Buraq Space Camp website — a small way to give back to a program that helped shape me.

Where the curiosity started

Before all of it, in school: selected for SUPARCO's Space Quiz during Space Week, a model rocket-launching competition, and a national spelling bee. I've been chasing this stuff for a long time.
