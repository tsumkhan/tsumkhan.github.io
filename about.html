About me.

An electrical and computer engineer who kept gravitating toward the point where software meets real hardware.

I'm from Karachi. I started university at 17 on a full-tuition merit scholarship at Habib University, studied electrical engineering, and kept gravitating toward the parts of the field where software meets real hardware — microcontrollers, sensors, control systems, and robots that have to work in the messy physical world.

Along the way I spent a summer as a fully-funded research fellow at Purdue University, built a self-balancing robot and a vision-guided robotic arm, published my final-year work in IEEE, and took a fish-waste-to-biodiesel idea to a global second-place finish at Texas A&M. After graduating I moved into industry at House of Habib — battery-chemistry research, a locally-designed transformer oil-level sensor, and a megawatt-scale solar installation among other projects.

It paid well and it was interesting. But I missed the hands-on research and teaching, so I came back to Habib University as a Research Assistant. Now I teach the microcontroller and robotics labs, mentor competition teams, and I'm working toward graduate research in embedded and cyber-physical systems. If you're looking for someone who is genuinely excited to build, break, and understand hardware — that's the whole reason I'm here.

Beyond the lab

The things that shaped how I think about engineering.

U.S.–Pakistan energy policy, in the room

As a member of the Pakistan-U.S. Alumni Network (PUAN), I was selected to attend Powering Progress: U.S.–Pakistan Synergy in Energy and Mineral Resources in Islamabad, alongside academics, policymakers, and industry leaders. We discussed Pakistan's underused energy potential and how to make the grid robust enough for large-scale renewable integration — the exact challenge I'd explored in my capstone. I also visited NEECA to hear their plans for national EV-charging infrastructure.

Buraq Space Camp, at 16

I went to a government college to save money — and still got selected as one of 40 students, out of more than 5,000 nationwide, for the prestigious Buraq Space Camp in 2018: ten residential days in Islamabad packed with lectures, activities, and meetings with professors. The highlight was meeting Dr. Pervez Hoodbhoy.

Fun fact: last year I helped build the Buraq Space Camp website — a small way to give back to a program that helped shape me.

Where the curiosity started

Before all of it, in school: selected for SUPARCO's Space Quiz during Space Week, a model rocket-launching competition, and a national spelling bee. I've been chasing this stuff for a long time.
